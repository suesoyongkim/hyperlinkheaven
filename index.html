LONG LIVE THE
WORLD WIDE WEB!

THE WEB WAS A PLACE TO MAKE YOURSELF AT HOME.

THE HISTORY FILE .TXT

1989. At CERN, Tim Berners-Lee proposed a way to connect and share documents through links. The World Wide Web was born from that idea. In 1993, CERN made the web software freely available, helping it spread beyond research labs.

1994. A free homepage service began as Beverly Hills Internet, soon known as GeoCities. People moved into themed virtual neighborhoods and decorated pages with animated GIFs, guestbooks, counters, MIDI music, and whatever else they loved.

1999–2009. Yahoo acquired GeoCities in 1999. The main English-language service closed in October 2009. Volunteers saved enormous collections of pages, preserving traces of a web made by everyday people.

WHY I LOVE IT .TXT

I love the web that looked like somebody made it. Pages with too many GIFs, tiny buttons, strange colors, and guestbooks full of strangers. You could leave a link to a friend, put your favorite music on repeat, and make a place that didn't need a reason to exist.

Not every corner had to be useful. A homepage could be unfinished, silly, deeply personal, and beautiful in its own way. The web wasn't just something to scroll through. It was something you could build, decorate, and give to someone else.

HYPERLINK HEAVEN is my little thank-you note to that idea. Here's to hand-coded pages, open doors, broken links, little GIFs, and the people who keep making rooms for themselves on the internet.

LONG LIVE THE PERSONAL HOMEPAGE. LONG LIVE THE WORLD WIDE WEB. :-)
